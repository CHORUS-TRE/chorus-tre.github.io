# About CHORUS.HIP

<Accordion summaryStyle={{ fontSize: '1.25rem', fontWeight: 'bold' }}>

<AccordionSection title="What is CHORUS.HIP?">

**CHORUS.HIP** is a scientific environment dedicated to intracerebral EEG, neuroimaging, and related clinical neuroscience research.

It brings together intracerebral EEG, neuroimaging, specialized neuroscience applications, and collaborative workflows within a secure environment built on **CHORUS**. CHORUS provides the underlying platform capabilities for secure access, workspaces, sessions, data management, and governance, while CHORUS.HIP focuses on the scientific workflows, datasets, applications, and practices specific to this research community.

> To learn more about the underlying platform, see the [**CHORUS documentation**](/docs/about).

Its focus is on what researchers need to do: prepare and curate data, organize it using standards such as BIDS-iEEG, localize implanted electrodes, analyse signals, combine electrophysiology with imaging, and collaborate across institutions within governed research projects.

**Scientific scope**

CHORUS.HIP is centred on intracerebral EEG and stereo-electroencephalography (SEEG), while also supporting the broader research context in which these recordings are used.

This includes epilepsy research, associated neuroimaging, electrode localization and anatomical labelling, multimodal clinical neuroscience, BIDS and BIDS-iEEG data organization, signal analysis, and computational neuroscience.

The aim is to bring these elements into a common scientific environment rather than forcing researchers to assemble separate tools, datasets, and transfer procedures for each project.

**Collaborative research, data, and applications**

Intracerebral EEG recordings are relatively rare and distributed across specialized clinical centres, making collaboration particularly important. CHORUS.HIP creates a common environment in which research teams can work across institutions, curate data toward shared standards, apply reproducible workflows, and analyse datasets made available under the relevant conditions.

Data and applications are brought together in the same research context, so researchers can move from curation and quality control to analysis and interpretation without relying on fragmented local environments. The available tools support the different stages of these workflows, while the documentation connects each application to the scientific task it is intended to support.

</AccordionSection>

<AccordionSection title="Working in CHORUS.HIP">

Research in CHORUS.HIP is organized around **projects, workspaces, sessions, datasets, and applications**.

These elements define where users work, who they collaborate with, and which data and tools are available in a given research context.

> To learn more about the underlying CHORUS concepts and platform model, see the [**CHORUS documentation**](/docs/about).

**Projects**

A **project** represents a defined research activity and provides the context for collaboration.

Projects can involve researchers from one or several institutions and bring together the people, datasets, and resources required for a specific scientific objective.

Examples include a multicentre SEEG study, development of an iEEG analysis method, or creation of a curated BIDS-iEEG dataset.

**Workspaces**

Research takes place in **workspaces**.

**Private workspace**

A private workspace is associated with an individual user and supports work that does not require a shared project environment.

**Project workspace**

A project workspace is associated with a research project and is shared by authorized project members.

It supports collaborative activities such as data curation, shared analyses, and reproducible research workflows.

**Sessions**

Applications are accessed through **sessions** started within a workspace.

A session is the running environment in which a researcher uses an application, for example to review iEEG recordings, process neuroimaging data, work in a programming environment, or run an analysis pipeline.

**Datasets and applications**

CHORUS.HIP brings scientific data and the applications used to work with it into the same research environment.

Datasets may include intracerebral EEG recordings, MRI or CT imaging, electrode information, clinical research metadata, BIDS-iEEG datasets, and derived research data. Access to a dataset depends on the user's permissions, the project in which the work is carried out, and the conditions associated with that dataset.

Applications support the different stages of the research workflow. Depending on the task, researchers may use tools for:

- iEEG visualization and signal analysis;
- BIDS and BIDS-iEEG preparation and validation;
- neuroimaging processing;
- SEEG electrode localization and anatomical labelling;
- programming and statistical analysis;
- computational modelling.

Applications are accessed through sessions within a workspace, allowing researchers to work with the relevant data without moving the scientific workflow outside the CHORUS.HIP environment.

The available datasets and applications may vary according to the project, user permissions, and current CHORUS.HIP deployment.

</AccordionSection>

<AccordionSection title="Governance and Responsible Use">

CHORUS.HIP is designed for research involving sensitive biomedical data, where scientific access must remain aligned with institutional, ethical, and legal requirements.

Governance is therefore part of the research environment itself. Access to projects, datasets, workspaces, and outputs is controlled according to the permissions and conditions that apply to each research activity. CHORUS provides the underlying mechanisms for access control, traceability, and governed data use, while CHORUS.HIP applies them in the context of intracerebral EEG and related neuroscience research.

> To learn more about the CHORUS security and governance model, see the [**CHORUS documentation**](/docs/about).

**Access follows the research context**

Access in CHORUS.HIP is not automatically granted to all resources.

A researcher may be a member of the CHORUS.HIP community while having access only to specific projects, workspaces, or datasets. These permissions reflect the research purpose, the user's role, and the conditions associated with the data.

This allows collaboration to take place within a defined research context rather than through unrestricted sharing.

**Data remains governed**

Datasets used in CHORUS.HIP remain subject to the authority and conditions established by the responsible institution or data controller.

Depending on the dataset and project, these conditions may determine:

- who can access the data;
- the purposes for which it can be used;
- the duration of access;
- how the data can be processed;
- which results may leave the controlled environment.

CHORUS.HIP does not replace these governance decisions. It creates an environment in which they can be applied and enforced during the research process.

**Responsible use of sensitive data**

Researchers are expected to work only with the data and resources for which they are authorized and within the scope of the approved research activity.

Sensitive data should remain within the controlled research environment unless an approved process allows otherwise. Activities performed within the platform can be recorded and audited, supporting accountability throughout the research lifecycle.

This approach reflects the broader **Five Safes** principles: trusted users working on appropriate projects, with suitable data, in controlled settings, and with appropriate safeguards around outputs.

**Responsible data reuse**

For sensitive neuroscience data, responsible reuse does not necessarily mean making data openly available.

CHORUS.HIP supports a model in which data can remain protected while still being made findable, interoperable, and reusable under appropriate access conditions. Standards such as BIDS-iEEG help improve consistency and interoperability, while access remains governed according to the sensitivity of the data and the permissions attached to it.

Raw data, derived data, metadata, and aggregated results may therefore be subject to different access and reuse conditions.

</AccordionSection>
